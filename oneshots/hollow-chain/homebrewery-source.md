The town has arrested a kind-hearted baker, Tomas Greaves, and he is on trial for his life. Lord Xanthos believes Tomas is innocent and sends the party to find the real killer before the town hangs the wrong man.</p><h3>What is really happening</h3><p>The killer is not a person. A few drops of black, oily life from another plane arrived by accident in a merchant’s sealed curio, which broke. It slips into a person through mouth, nose or wound, wears them like a coat and eats them from the inside, while they stay awake and helpless. When the body is empty, it moves on. It has fed fifteen times and grows hungrier with every meal.</p></div><div><h3>Where it leads</h3><ul><li><strong>Act I:</strong> the trial, the coroner’s report, Harbour Street and a strange hunger.</li><li><strong>Act II:</strong> Edric Thorne and the thing inside him, a chase through the drainage channels, and the gallows.</li><li><strong>Act III:</strong> the creature bursts out in the town square and seizes four townspeople. Free them, and kill it.</li><li><strong>Ending:</strong> smoke, survivors, and a magistrate who must answer for what he did.</li></ul><p>Built for six level-5 characters in one session of about 3–3½ hours. <h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>An accident.</strong> A travelling merchant sold curiosities in Wendle Staithe last season. Among them was a small sealed vessel holding a few drops of something black and oily, alive and from another plane. Nobody summoned it. The vessel cracked, and it got out.</li>
<li><strong>What it is.</strong> One hungry creature, driven by instinct. It is not clever and has no plan. It enters a person through the mouth, nose or an open wound and <strong>takes over their body completely</strong>: their walk, voice and face. Inside, it eats them. <strong>The person stays awake</strong>, unable to move or speak for themselves. When nothing is left but a dried shell, it pours out and finds someone else.</li>
<li><strong>It is growing.</strong> It began as a few grams. After fifteen meals it weighs about as much as a child, though it can swell and stretch to look far bigger. The gaps between deaths have shrunk from seven days to about three.</li>
<li><strong>The hunger.</strong> When it first takes a body, it makes its host eat ravenously for a day. That is the clue that cracks the case.</li>
<li><strong>What it isn’t.</strong> It doesn’t eat souls, as far as anyone can tell. The faces and voices it copies are imitations, not trapped people. It can’t read minds.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The fixed rules</h3>

<p><strong>Wendle Staithe</strong> is a market town on a slow river, all brick warehouses, tarred fishing boats and a sloping cobbled square with a stone market cross. Rain runs off its streets into <strong>open drainage channels</strong>: stone-lined ditches a yard wide, crossing under the lanes through short culverts and emptying into the river. <strong>There are no sewers.</strong></p>
<p>Fifteen people have died in two or three months, more often lately. The town is frightened and angry, and it wants someone hanged. The magistrate has given them a baker.</p>

<li><strong>Pacing:</strong> Act I about 70 minutes, Act II about 70, Act III about 60.</li>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>A <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>, five or six levels above the party, measured and courteous, with a dry wit. He is <strong>Lord of Silverwood</strong>: famous here, but with no power over this town’s court. Ask his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> Of Silverwood. You will have heard of it, if not of me.”</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Says he’s doing:</strong> finding a killer, and keeping an innocent man alive.</li>
<li><strong>Privately:</strong> Coroner Marrable wondered in her report whether <em>even the soul</em> had been taken. That question is why he came.</li>
<li><strong>What he believes:</strong> a person is doing this, by forbidden means. He wants them <strong>caught alive and questioned</strong>. He has no idea it is a creature.</li>
<li><strong>Why he leaves:</strong> the earlier bodies are at Fennick Cross. He goes to examine them himself. It is real work, not an excuse.</li>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><span>The Hollow Chain</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:130px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/hollow-chain/img/banner-act1.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h2>Act I — The Accused</h2>
<p><em>About 70 minutes.</em></p>
<h3>Lord Xanthos's briefing</h3>
<p>He meets the party at the inn on the square, in a private room, before they go near the court.</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>Lord Xanthos stands at the window, watching the crowd outside the courthouse. He does not turn round at once.</em></p>
<p><em>“I asked for investigators before they arrested anyone,” he says. “I arrive, and they are trying a baker.” He turns. “I questioned Master Greaves this morning, under a spell that does not permit lies. I am satisfied he has killed no one.</em></p>
<p><em>“Do not tell the magistrate. Whoever is doing this believes they have got away with it, and people who believe that grow careless. I want them alive. I have questions.” A pause. “And I would prefer it if the town did not hang the wrong man while I ask them.”</em></p></blockquote><p>He gives them <strong>Coroner Marrable’s report</strong> (the handout) and leaves for <strong>Fennick Cross</strong> that afternoon, to examine the earlier bodies. He will return as soon as he can.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>What he can tell them</h3>
<ul>
<li>Tomas answered every question under <em>Zone of Truth</em>. Xanthos believes him, but <strong>a spell that stops lies doesn’t make a man right about everything</strong>. Tomas could be mistaken, and Xanthos knows it.</li>
<li>The victims were emptied <strong>without a mark on the outside</strong>. Xanthos has never seen anything like it.</li>
<li>He thinks a person is doing this. <strong>He is wrong</strong>, and the party will find out first.</li>
</ul>
<h3>The report</h3>
<p>Let the players read the handout. Its key points:</p>
<ul>
<li>Skin dried and unmarked. Inside, <strong>perfectly smooth</strong>: no blood, flesh or organs.</li>
<li>The bodies are so dry that <strong>no one can tell when they died</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>No residue of any kind.</strong></li>
<li>The coroner’s note: <em>“One is tempted to wonder whether even the soul was taken.”</em></li>
</ul><div class="gm-note"><h4>No automatic acquittal</h4><p>Xanthos’s belief is not evidence the court will accept. Nothing in Act I frees Tomas on its own. The party are building a case they will need at the gallows.</p></div></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Hollow Chain</span><span>5</span></div>

<p><em>The baker says nothing at all.</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>Magistrate Osric Penhallow</strong> is not corrupt. He is frightened, exhausted and under huge pressure: fifteen dead, and a town that wants someone to blame. Tomas is the only suspect he has. He trusts them more if they say Xanthos sent them.</p>
<p><strong>Why he’s silent:</strong> he takes bread and grain to the poor of <strong>Harbour Street</strong>, in secret, so as not to shame them. He won’t name them in open court.</p>
<p><strong>What he tells the party</strong> (no roll if they’re kind; DC 13 Persuasion if not):</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>“Widow Hesketh. Tuesday last, she ate everything I brought, all of it, standing up. Like she’d not eaten in a week. And old Jory Rudd, next door, the day after, the same. Starving. Couldn’t stop.</em></p>
<p><em>“I went back to the widow on the Thursday.” He swallows. “She was… empty. Like a dried leaf.”</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>This is the key clue.</strong> Ravenous hunger comes first; a husk follows a few days later. And <strong>Jory Rudd</strong> is now dead too.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Hollow Chain</span><span>6</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><span>The Hollow Chain</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The investigation</h3>
<p>Let the party split up and follow leads. Each gives <strong>evidence</strong> for the gallows; note what they find. None needs a single make-or-break roll: if a check fails, the clue costs time, or a coin, or a rude conversation.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Harbour Street.</strong> The poor confirm Tomas’s secret visits and kindness. Neighbours of Jory Rudd saw him “eating like a wolf” the day before he died, and <strong>Tomas wasn’t there that day</strong>. <em>Evidence: the hunger pattern.</em></li>
<li><strong>The dead, in order.</strong> The parish clerk’s list (DC 12 Investigation): gaps of about 7, 6, 5, 4, then 3 days. <strong>Several deaths fall on days Tomas was baking for the fair.</strong> <em>Evidence: the timeline.</em></li>
<li><strong>Tomas’s ledger.</strong> In the bakery, a flour-dusted book of who got what, when. It matches the visits, nothing more. <em>Backs up his story.</em></li>
<li><strong>The sixteenth body.</strong> Found while Tomas is in the cells: a lamplighter, dried hollow. The magistrate says he could have died weeks ago. A DC 14 Medicine check finds a half-eaten pie in his pocket, <strong>still fresh</strong>. He died this week. <em>Evidence: the fresh pie.</em></li>
<li><strong>The merchant.</strong> Market folk remember a curio-seller last season, and a cracked jar he threw away by the channels (DC 13 Investigation, or buy a drink). <em>Evidence: the jar.</em></li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:210px"><img alt="At Fennick Cross, Lord Xanthos examines the dead." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/hollow-chain/img/husk.jpg" style="object-position:50% 45%"></div><figcaption>At Fennick Cross, Lord Xanthos examines the dead.</figcaption></figure><h3>The channels</h3>
<p>On the way somewhere, the party pass the washing steps where women do laundry in the clean upstream channel. <strong>Nell Hartwell</strong>, a sharp-tongued washerwoman, shouts at them for treading on her sheets, then gossips: “<em>You’re the ones looking into the husks? Ask about Edric Thorne. Carter. Ate a whole ham at the Anchor two nights back. Whole ham, bone and all, near enough.</em>”</p>
<p><strong>Introduce Nell clearly.</strong> The party need to know her face. She becomes the creature’s host in Act III.</p>
<h3>Pressure</h3>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><span>The Hollow Chain</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:130px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/hollow-chain/img/banner-end.jpg" style="object-position:50% 55%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h2>Ending — Smoke Over the Square</h2><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The smoke drifts away over the rooftops. Somewhere a child is crying, and somewhere else a woman is laughing because she is alive, and the whole square stands staring at a black stain on the cobbles where the thing used to be.</em></p>
<p><em>Hooves clatter on the bridge. Lord Xanthos rides into the square, his horse lathered, and takes it in with a single look: the stain, the wounded, the gallows.</em></p>
<p><em>He is off his horse before it has stopped, and kneeling beside the nearest of the injured.</em></p></blockquote><h3>Xanthos returns</h3>
<p>He helps the local healers with the survivors, <strong>calmly and well</strong>. His magic keeps the dying alive and closes wounds. <strong>It does not regrow what was eaten.</strong> He is gentle with the townsfolk and very interested in what happened to them.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Nell Hartwell</strong> survives only if the party freed her before her feeds ran out. Even then, she is badly hurt. Xanthos’s help gives her a fighting chance; tell the players she will live, but never fully recover.</li>
<li><strong>Edric Thorne</strong>, if he lived, dies peacefully within a few days, with Xanthos sitting by him, asking gentle questions.</li>
<li>If a <strong>sample</strong> survived, Xanthos takes it with great care. If not, he is visibly disappointed, then says: “<em>The town is alive. I can live without a specimen.</em>”</li>
<li><strong>His question about souls stays unanswered.</strong> He will not say what he thinks.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The magistrate</h3>
<p>How Penhallow behaves depends on <strong>what the party did</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>They proved it with evidence:</strong> he apologises to Tomas in front of the whole town, and means it.</li>
<li><strong>They rescued Tomas by force:</strong> he is defensive. “<em>The evidence was clear. Who is to say the baker didn’t call the thing up?</em>” The party may have to keep arguing.</li>
<li><strong>Tomas was hanged:</strong> he says in public that he acted rightly. In private, he cannot meet anyone’s eye, and he resigns within the year.</li>
</ul>

<li>Xanthos is away until the end.</li>

<p>The square fight works without a map if you keep the <strong>captives</strong> in front of the players. Write the four names on cards, with their feeds, and turn one down each round.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The creature</strong> fills the middle of the square, up to fifteen feet tall when swollen. Say how tall it is now: it shrinks when hurt.</li>
<li><strong>The captives</strong> hang at different heights. Nell is in the middle; the others swing out at the ends of long tendrils.</li>
<li><strong>The crowd</strong> scatters into doorways, the inn, behind the market cross. Nobody else needs to be hurt unless the party drive it towards them.</li>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><span>The Hollow Chain</span><span>PLAYER HANDOUT</span></div><div class="pf-body columns handout-page oo-prop" style="--accent:#5c3d2e"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><p class="map-eyebrow">PLAYER HANDOUT 1</p><h3>The coroner’s report</h3><p><strong>To Lord Xanthos of Silverwood, from Ysolde Marrable, Coroner of Fennick Cross.</strong></p>
<p>My lord. Seven of the dead from Wendle Staithe have been brought to me. I set down what I have found.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The skin is dry and unbroken.</strong> No wound, no puncture, no stretching or tearing. The bodies have not been opened.</li>
<li><strong>The insides are empty.</strong> Blood, fat, muscle, organs, sinew: all gone. What remains is <strong>smooth</strong>, as if polished.</li>
<li><strong>I cannot say when they died.</strong> They are so dry that a body a week dead looks like one dead a season.</li>
<li><strong>There is no residue of any kind.</strong> No stain, no powder, no smell beyond the ordinary.</li>
</ol>
<p>I know of no disease, poison, beast or spell that does this.</p>
<p><em>One is tempted to wonder whether even the soul was taken. I set that down only because I cannot stop thinking it.</em></p>
