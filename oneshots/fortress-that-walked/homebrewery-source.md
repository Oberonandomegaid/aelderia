It sits on the back of a colossal, ancient tortoise, and goblins are steering it. Lord Xanthos lifts the beast just off the ground so it can’t walk, but holding it takes everything he has. The party ride floating stones up onto the shell, fight their way through the goblin-held fortress, and bring down the Dark Goblin King.</p><h3>What is really happening</h3><p>A lord built Harrowgate Keep over ten years on the only hill that never moved. Then the hill woke up and everyone fled. Goblins moved in during its next long sleep. Now the tortoise is awake and starving, and the goblins drive it into farmland with dangled food and thorn whips on its neck. The tortoise isn’t attacking anyone. It’s eating. It is innocent, and it must survive.</p></div><div><h3>Where it leads</h3><ul><li><strong>Act I:</strong> Titan’s Suspension, the floating stones, a fight with the sentries.</li><li><strong>Act II:</strong> two routes in: along the neck to cut the whips, or through the front gate and great hall.</li><li><strong>Act III:</strong> the Dark Goblin King on the top tower, throwing his dinner at people.</li><li><strong>Ending:</strong> the goblins scatter; Lord Xanthos lowers the tortoise and leads it to good grazing.</li></ul><p>Built for six level-5 characters in one session of about 3–3½ hours. <h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>The tortoise is a living wonder.</strong> It is older than any record, sleeps for decades at a time, and lets grass, trees and even soil grow right to the edge of its shell. Asleep, it looks exactly like a hill.</li>
<li><strong>The fortress was a mistake.</strong> A regional lord spent ten years building Harrowgate Keep on it, to control the Rolling Hills. When it woke and walked, the garrison ran for their lives.</li>
<li><strong>The goblins found it later</strong>, during another long sleep, and have used it ever since as a walking stronghold for raids.</li>
<li><strong>They steer it badly.</strong> Bait carts of hay hang off the shell in front of its nose, and goblins on the neck lash it with thorn whips. It goes where the food is, and away from the pain. They don’t control its mind.</li>
<li><strong>The tortoise is just hungry.</strong> It doesn’t want to flatten Furrowmoot. Furrowmoot is simply where the crops are.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The fixed rules</h3>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5a4a2a"><span>The Fortress That Walked</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5a4a2a"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:170px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/fortress-that-walked/img/banner-setting.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The setting</h3>
<p>The <strong>Rolling Hills</strong> live up to their name. The ground here moves: slowly, over seasons, the hills drift and swell and settle, so that maps go out of date and villages move their fences every spring. Nothing can be built to last. The nomads who cross them carry their homes in carts.</p>
<p>So when a lord found one hill that stayed put, year after year, he did what lords do. He built a castle on it. <strong>Harrowgate Keep</strong> took ten years: curtain walls, a great hall, a gatehouse, a tall tower. The hill was the back of a tortoise the size of a mountain, and one morning it opened its eyes.</p>
<p><strong>Furrowmoot</strong> is a farming village of barley fields and stone barns at the edge of the Hills. Today a castle is walking towards it, munching hedgerows.</p>

<li><strong>Pacing:</strong> Act I about 50 minutes, Act II about 85, Act III about 50.</li>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>A <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>, five or six levels above the party, measured and authoritative, and an aristocrat to the bone. Ask his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> That will do.”</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Says he’s doing:</strong> saving Furrowmoot.</li>
<li><strong>Privately interested in:</strong> how anything lives this long, and sleeps that deeply. He watches the tortoise the way a jeweller watches a diamond.</li>
<li><strong>Never:</strong> leaves the suspension, takes the party’s fight, or talks about himself.</li>
</ul>
<p>Today he is holding a mountain in the air with one hand and gives the impression this is beneath him. Voice: a nobleman made to carry his own luggage.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Fortress That Walked</span><span>4</span></div>

<p><em>About 50 minutes.</em></p>
<h3>Furrowmoot</h3>
<p>The party arrive at Furrowmoot answering a call for help. The village is emptying: carts piled with furniture, children carrying chickens, and every face turned west, where a <strong>hill is walking</strong>.</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>On the horizon, a green hill is moving. As it comes closer you see the castle on top: walls, a gatehouse, a tall tower flying a ragged black flag. Beneath it, under a fringe of grass and dangling roots, a head the size of a barn swings slowly from side to side, tearing up a hedgerow with every bite.</em></p>
<p><em>A stout woman with a pitchfork marches up to you. “Hedda Plowright. Headwoman. That,” she jabs the fork at the horizon, “is eating my barley. And there are goblins on it.”</em></p></blockquote><h3>Hedda Plowright</h3>
<p>Broad, sunburnt, loud. Furious rather than frightened. She’ll offer <strong>150 gp</strong> from the village chest, and adds, darkly, that the goblins took three sheep and her best cart last time. She has hay, rope and a ladder, and she is not leaving.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:400px"><img alt="Lord Xanthos, 905 THK." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/fortress-that-walked/img/xanthos-portrait.jpg" style="object-position:50% 0%"></div><figcaption>Lord Xanthos, 905 THK.</figcaption></figure><h3>Lord Xanthos</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>A man in plain dark robes is standing alone in the path of the walking hill, perfectly calm. He doesn’t look round.</em></p>
<p><em>“You are the help, I take it. Good.” He studies the tortoise with open fascination. “Do you know how old that animal is? No. Neither does anyone else.”</em></p></blockquote><p>If asked his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong>” As if it settles everything.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Fortress That Walked</span><span>5</span></div>

It is not malicious. It is hungry, and it is going where they point it, which is through this village.</em></p>
<p><em>“I am going to lift it. Not far. Enough that its feet cannot find the ground. While I hold it there, it goes nowhere. But I will be holding it, and I will not be able to do anything else.</em></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>“Can’t you just blast the castle?”</strong> “And the tortoise underneath it? No.”</li>
<li><strong>“How long can you hold it?”</strong> “Long enough. Do not test that.”</li>
<li><strong>“How do we get up?”</strong> He gestures, mildly. See <em>Titan’s Suspension</em>.</li>
<li><strong>“Why do you care about the tortoise?”</strong> “Because nothing else alive has seen what it has seen.” A pause. “And because killing it would be wasteful.”</li>

With a groan like a ship’s timbers, the whole hill lifts: grass, trees, castle and all, a handspan off the ground. Its great feet paddle slowly at nothing. A deep, puzzled rumble comes out of the tortoise.</em></p>
<p><em>Slabs of stone tear free from the ground and float up around the shell, turning slowly, making a crooked stair into the sky.</em></p>
<p><em>“Up you go,” says Lord Xanthos, through gritted teeth.</em></p></blockquote><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:200px;margin-top:8px"><img alt="Titan’s Suspension and the floating stones" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/fortress-that-walked/img/suspension.jpg" style="object-position:50% 45%"></figure><p><strong>The floating stones.</strong> Each is a few strides across and drifts gently. Jumping between them takes no check unless someone is showing off or fighting on them (DC 10 Dex (Acrobatics) to jump while burdened). A fall drops you safely onto the grass below; climb again.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Fortress That Walked</span><span>6</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5a4a2a"><span>The Fortress That Walked</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5a4a2a"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The shell rim</h3>
<p>The floating stones bring the party up onto the <strong>shell rim</strong>: a ring of rough turf and scrub between the shell’s edge and the castle walls. The goblins have noticed that their castle has stopped walking and started floating.</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>You step off the last stone onto grass. It is a hill, truly a hill: turf, gorse, a crooked hawthorn. Then the ground under your boots shifts, and lifts, and settles, like the deck of a ship. Somewhere far beneath you, something very large sighs.</em></p>
<p><em>Shrieks from the walls. Goblins are pouring out of a sally door, worgs at their heels.</em></p></blockquote><h3>The sentries</h3>
<p><strong>2 goblin bosses, 8 goblin warriors and 4 worgs.</strong> About 1,200 XP: an easy warm-up. Keep it to 15–20 minutes, noisy and fun. Use one Shell Shift in the middle of it, to teach the rule.</p><div class="gm-note"><h4>Shell Shift</h4><p>When the tortoise lurches, every creature standing on the shell, goblins and King included, makes a <b>DC 12 Dexterity saving throw</b>. <em>Failure:</em> it falls Prone. <em>Success:</em> it keeps its footing. Nobody takes damage or falls off the shell. Use it now and then, at dramatic moments, never every round.</p></div></div><div class="flow-col"><div class="combat-note"><h4>Encounter · GM reference</h4><p><strong>Terrain.</strong> Turf, gorse bushes (Half Cover) and the hawthorn. The castle wall is 30 ft. high, with a sally door 20 ft. away. The shell edge drops off 40 ft. to the ground: a creature shoved over lands safely on the grass below and has to climb back up a floating stone.</p>
<p><strong>Tactics.</strong> The worgs charge; the goblins shoot from the gorse and use Nimble Escape to hide. The bosses swap places with warriors when hit, cackling.</p>
<p><strong>When it turns.</strong> When both bosses fall, the rest bolt back inside, shrieking “THEY’RE ON THE HILL!” Every goblin in the keep now knows.</p></div><h3>Two ways in</h3>
<p>Once the sentries are gone, the party can see their two options (show the map, page 13):</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Route A, the neck.</strong> Along the rim to the front of the shell, where the great neck stretches out. Goblins on platforms there are lashing it with thorn whips. Past them, a rear postern into the keep.</li>
<li><strong>Route B, the gate.</strong> Straight round to the front gatehouse and through the great hall. Heavily defended, but direct.</li>
</ul>

<p>Not tragic, not misunderstood: a clever, greedy, self-satisfied conqueror who has done very well out of a walking castle and fully intends to keep it. <div class="running" style="--accent:#5a4a2a"><span>The Fortress That Walked</span><span>GM · THEATRE OF THE MIND</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5a4a2a"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h2>Theatre of the mind</h2>
<p>The fortress works well without a map. Keep three things clear: <strong>how high</strong>, <strong>how close to the edge</strong>, and <strong>which way is downhill right now</strong>.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The shell</strong> is a tilted world. After every Shell Shift, tell the players which way is downhill. Anything round rolls that way.</li>
<li><strong>The neck</strong> is a moving road with a long drop either side. Name each whip platform (“the near one, the one with the fat goblin, the far one by the ear”) so players can pick targets.</li>
<li><strong>The tower</strong> is a round stage: throne at the back, drum on the left, brazier on the right, the drop all around.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><table>
