<h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>The Warden was a guardian, not a monster.</strong> For centuries it kept the Lantern Barrows: tended the lamps, sealed the tombs, sang the dead to rest. Somewhere in that long watch, duty became grief, and grief became a conviction: that death is abandonment, and that it could save them.</li>
<li><strong>Its resurrections are flawed.</strong> The risen are truly conscious. They speak, remember and recognise people. But each is trapped in its last moments: the drowned keep gasping, the soldier keeps bracing for the charge. The Warden can’t, or won’t, see it.</li>
<li><strong>It lost control, not its mind.</strong> It pushed its magic further to perfect it, and the magic spilled beyond the Barrows. Now the dead rise without end and march on Ashwell Cross. The Warden still chooses everything it does.</li>
<li><strong>No one is pulling its strings.</strong> No mastermind, no possession. A sincere guardian, a terrible mistake, and the will to keep making it.</li>

<p><strong>Ashwell Cross</strong> is a market town of grey stone and thatch where two old roads meet, a day’s ride from anywhere important. Its wealth is wool, its pride is its bridge, and its oldest neighbour is the <strong>Lantern Barrows</strong>: a hill riddled with tombs, a mile east, where the region has buried its dead for longer than anyone has records.</p>
<p>The Barrows are named for their lamps. Green glass lanterns hang at every tomb door, and for as long as the town remembers they have burned without oil. Three nights ago they all flared at once. The next night, the first of the dead walked out.</p>
<p>Now the road east is full of them: farmers, soldiers, children in their burial clothes, bones in rags. The town has thrown up barricades of carts and timber. They won’t hold for long on their own.</p>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>He is a <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>: far more powerful than the party (think five or six levels above them), sincerely protective, measured and authoritative. He is also an aristocrat to his bones. Ask his name and he says, “Lord Xanthos,” in a tone that makes clear that is all someone of your standing requires.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>What he says he’s doing:</strong> saving Ashwell Cross. He means it.</li>
<li><strong>What interests him privately:</strong> how memory and personality survive in the risen. He may ask an unusually sharp question about it. Nothing more.</li>
<li><strong>What he never does:</strong> take the party’s fight, explain himself at length, or reveal anything of his own history.</li>
</ul>
<p>Voice: a duke reviewing troops he didn’t choose but has decided to keep alive.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Dead Shall Rise</span><span>4</span></div>

<p><em>About 60 minutes.</em></p>
<h3>The barricade at dusk</h3>
<p>The party arrives at Ashwell Cross in the late afternoon, answering the reeve’s call for swords. The east road is a wall of overturned carts, barrels and torn-up fencing, manned by farmers with pitchforks and a handful of guards. A mile away, on the hill, green lights burn.</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The town smells of smoke and fear. Behind the barricade, a woman in a mud-spattered coat is shouting orders that nobody quite obeys. On the cart beside her, utterly still, stands a man in fine robes, looking east as if the whole road were his to inspect.</em></p>
<p><em>He turns his head. His gaze moves over you once, measures you, and settles.</em></p>
<p><em>“Ah. The adventurers. Good. You are later than I would have liked, but you are here, and that will have to do.”</em></p></blockquote><p>If anyone asks who he is: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong>” A slight pause. “I imagine that will suffice.”</p></div><div class="flow-col"><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:400px"><img alt="Lord Xanthos, 870 THK." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/dead-shall-rise/img/xanthos-portrait.jpg" style="object-position:50% 0%"></div><figcaption>Lord Xanthos, 870 THK.</figcaption></figure><h3>The briefing</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>“The dead are coming out of the Barrows, and they are not stopping. <p><em>“Something in that hill is raising them, and it is not doing so by accident. Until it is ended, this town has perhaps two nights.</em></p>
<p><em>“I can hold that road. I cannot hold it and walk into the Barrows at the same time. You can. When the sun sets, I will open you a way through. You will go in, find whatever is responsible, and destroy it.”</em></p></blockquote></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Dead Shall Rise</span><span>5</span></div>

<h3>Reeve Maude Kettering</h3>
<p>Broad, red-handed, a sheep farmer who became reeve because nobody else would. She is terrified and hides it under bossiness. She’ll give the party anything the town has: rope, lamps, a cart, holy water from the chapel (three flasks).</p><div class="gm-note"><h4>Holy water</h4><p>Each flask: throw it as an action (range 20 ft.); an undead hit takes 2d6 Radiant damage. The flames part down the middle like a curtain, leaving a lane of scorched earth running east into the dark.</em></p>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#3d4a3a"><span>The Dead Shall Rise</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#3d4a3a"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The road to the Barrows</h3>
<p>Halfway up the hill, where the lane passes an old roadside shrine, a knot of the dead has slipped through: a <strong>wight</strong> in rusted mail, leading <strong>4 ghouls</strong> and <strong>10 zombies</strong>. About 2,000 XP: an easy warm-up for six level-5 characters. Let it be loud, fast and fun, and keep it to 15–20 minutes. It should bloody them a little, not drain them.</p><div class="combat-note"><h4>Encounter · GM reference</h4><p><strong>Terrain.</strong> The scorched lane is 15 ft. wide; beyond it, the burning edge of Lord Xanthos’s fire (any creature that enters it or starts its turn there takes 2d6 Fire damage). The shrine gives Half Cover.</p>
<p><strong>Tactics.</strong> The zombies shamble straight in. The ghouls go for whoever is isolated. The wight hangs back with its bow, then wades in when a ghoul paralyses someone.</p>
<p><strong>When it turns.</strong> The dead don’t flee. When the wight falls, the rest keep fighting, but the lane is clear enough to run for the gates instead.</p></div><h3>Talking to the wight</h3>
<p>The wight was a soldier who died holding this very road. It thinks the party are raiders. “You’ll not pass. <h3>The Lantern Barrows</h3>
<p>The Barrows are a hill hollowed out like a honeycomb. A broad processional way climbs to the <strong>Gate</strong>, a stone arch flanked by green lanterns. Inside, the tunnels run downhill through five places that matter:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The Gate</strong>, where Corporal Pike still keeps watch.</li>
<li><strong>The Hall of Last Moments</strong>, a great vault where the newest risen wander.</li>
<li><strong>The Drowned Ossuary</strong>, where the deep tombs have flooded.</li>
<li><strong>The Lamp Gallery</strong>, a long hall of shelves and lanterns, guarded.</li>
<li><strong>The Warden's Workshop</strong>, its study, beside the stair to its chamber.</li>
</ol>
<p>Every tunnel is lit by those same green lamps: dim light everywhere, darkness in the side tombs. The air is cold and smells of wet stone and old incense. Distantly, always, the dead are murmuring.</p>
<p>The route is straightforward: the Barrows lead down, and down leads to the Warden. Let the party explore the side tombs if they like: roll on the side-tomb table (page 11) for each one they open.</p></p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The scorched lane ends at the foot of a great stone stair. <p><em>In front of the arch, on a wooden chair, sits a soldier with a spear.</em></p></blockquote></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>1 · The Forgotten Gatekeeper</h3><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:190px"><img alt="Corporal Edmer Pike, at his post." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/dead-shall-rise/img/pike.jpg" style="object-position:50% 18%"></div><figcaption>Corporal Edmer Pike, at his post.</figcaption></figure><p><strong>Corporal Edmer Pike</strong> died at this post a hundred and forty years ago. <p><em>“Evening. Barrows are closed after dusk, as a rule, but you look like you’ve business. Names and business, if you please, and mind the step, it’s worn.”</em></p></blockquote><ul>

<p><em>About 50 minutes.</em></p>
<h3>The Warden's chamber</h3>
<p>The stair spirals down into a round vault, ringed with open tombs and lit by a single great lantern hanging above a stone chair. <p><em>“Visitors. Living ones. You have come a long way through my halls. Did you see them? Did you see how they talk, and remember, and know their own names?</em></p>

<p>The Warden will talk, gladly. It wants to be understood. It is sincere, articulate and completely certain. If the party describe Honor, or Rook, or Ma Pennick, it answers: “<em>Yes. The flaw is in them. Not in the work. A little longer and they will be whole.</em>”</p>
<p>No speech ends this. A brilliant argument might make it pause, or grieve, or even agree they are suffering, but it will still not stop: “<em>Then I must try harder.</em>” When the party move against it, or its patience ends, it fights.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:200px"><img alt="The Broken Guardian." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/dead-shall-rise/img/guardian.jpg" style="object-position:50% 16%"></div><figcaption>The Broken Guardian.</figcaption></figure><h3>The fight</h3>
<p><strong>The Broken Guardian</strong>, with <strong>2 wights</strong> and <strong>2 ghasts</strong> at its side, raising the fallen as it goes. About 6,600 XP once its raised zombies join: a High fight. This is the big one.</p><div class="combat-note"><h4>Encounter · GM reference</h4><p><strong>Round one:</strong> the wights and ghasts step out of the tombs either side; the Guardian opens with Grave Chill on the biggest knot of characters.</p>
<p><strong>Every round:</strong> Raise the Fallen pulls up to two zombies out of the open tombs (there are plenty). The zombies are slow fodder; they exist to clog the room.</p>
<p><strong>The last light:</strong> the phylactery hangs 15 ft. up, above the chair. Anyone can reach it by climbing the chair (DC 12 Athletics) or with magic or a good throw of a rope. Taking it down costs an action. It can’t be broken by anything the party have: it is warded, and blows simply ring off it. The Guardian targets whoever holds it.</p>
<p><strong>At 0 Hit Points:</strong> see <em>When it falls</em>, overleaf.</p></div></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Dead Shall Rise</span><span>16</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#3d4a3a"><span>The Dead Shall Rise</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#3d4a3a"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>When it falls</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The Warden staggers. The green lights in its eyes flicker. It looks past you, up the stair, towards everything it raised.</em></p>
<p><em>“They were… so lonely,” it says.</em></p>
<p><em>Then it falls, and the whole hill sighs.</em></p>
<p><em>Every risen thing in the chamber drops where it stands. Far above, you hear it: a long rolling clatter, like a thousand puppets with their strings cut at once, running away through the halls and out onto the road.</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>Everything collapses at once.</strong> Every undead sustained by the Warden’s magic falls: the zombies here, the guards, the risen in the Hall, the horde on the road. <strong>Corporal Pike too</strong>, at his post, still upright on his chair, spear in hand. If the party befriended him, let them find him on their way out.</p>
<p>The green lanterns all through the Barrows go out, one by one, except the last light.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>Lord Xanthos arrives</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>Footsteps on the stair, unhurried. Lord Xanthos steps into the chamber, his robes scorched at the hem, not a hair out of place.</em></p>
<p><em>He takes in the room in a single glance: the fallen Warden, the lantern, you.</em></p>
<p><em>“Well,” he says. “You did not make me regret it.”</em></p></blockquote><p>He goes straight to the <strong>last light</strong>, wherever it is: still hanging, or in a character’s hands. He studies it for a long moment, longer than strictly necessary, and something unreadable crosses his face.</p>
<p>“<em>A prison that can hold a soul may also hold the hand that opens it.</em>” He speaks a single word. The lantern’s glass turns white, cracks, and goes dark. The Warden is finished.</p>
<p>If anyone asks what he learned, he answers carefully: “<em>That memory is more stubborn than flesh. That is all.</em>”</p><div class="gm-note"><h4>Keep his secrets</h4><p>He may show an unusual interest in how the risen kept their memories. He never explains why, and nothing in this adventure suggests anything more about him.</p></div></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Dead Shall Rise</span><span>17</span></div>

<p>The Barrows work well without a map. Keep three things in front of the players: <strong>how far</strong>, <strong>what’s in the way</strong>, and <strong>where the light is</strong>.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>On the road:</strong> the lane is narrow; the fire either side is the edge of the world. Count zombies out loud as they fall. It helps the warm-up feel like a horde.</li>
<li><strong>In the Gallery:</strong> describe the rows of shelves as lanes. Who is in which lane, who is behind a shelf, which specter just came through the wood.</li>
<li><strong>In the chamber:</strong> the room is a ring. The chair and the last light are the centre; the open tombs are the rim, and every round something climbs out of the rim.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><table>
