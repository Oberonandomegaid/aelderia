She hires adventurers with <strong>no ties to her house</strong> to collect a sealed strongbox after her death, keep the peace at the reading of her will and protect her granddaughter, <strong>Lilith</strong>.</p><p>Everyone assumes the box holds the Valtair black pearls and the family crest. Octavia never says so. It holds only her will, and the will skips a generation: almost everything goes to fourteen-year-old Lilith, who wants none of it.</p><h3>What is really happening</h3><p>The family’s politics turn to steel. On the road to the country house near Lindenvale, hired killers strike. Lilith’s mother takes the blow meant for her, and Lilith, holding her, reaches without thinking for the black pearl at her throat. The dead get up. Then the dead they kill get up. And it doesn’t stop until Lilith does.</p><p>The killers were sent by her own father.</p></div><div><h3>Where it leads</h3><ul><li><strong>Act 0:</strong> the Guild job; Octavia’s audience; meeting Lilith; a natural death.</li><li><strong>Act 1:</strong> the strongbox escort, a traitor in the escort and a choice of route home.</li><li><strong>Act 2:</strong> the will, read in full, and one furious exit.</li><li><strong>Act 3:</strong> out of Valenne: preparations that shape the finale.</li><li><strong>Act 4:</strong> the assassins, Eveline’s fall and the rising dead.</li></ul><p>Built for six level-5 characters in one session of about 3½ hours. <h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>The box holds only the will.</strong> No pearls, no crest, no false bottom.</li>
<li><strong>Lilith already wears the master pearl.</strong> Octavia gave it to her a month ago: a single black pearl in a silver clasp on a chain. It isn't drilled. Lilith thinks of it as a keepsake. Octavia told her the family history, Saraphina's story and what the pearls can do, but never how to use one.</li>
<li><strong>The master pearl is the one Vartius Xanthos secretly altered</strong> centuries ago, which made it the strongest of them all. None of this comes out here.</li>
<li><strong>The rest of the pearls</strong> are silver-mounted on a necklace, locked in a magical box beneath Saraphina's statue at the country estate.</li>
<li><strong>The crest seal</strong>, the key to that box, is sewn inside the stuffed toy Octavia gave Lilith. Nobody finds it in this adventure.</li>
<li><strong>Cassian sent the killers.</strong> Lilith's father hires the assassins who strike on the road, and his target is Eveline, not Lilith. With Eveline dead, the law makes him Lilith's guardian and the Valtair fortune is his to run. He means to let Lilith come of age, then put Tarquin, who does as he's told, or himself at the head of the house. Everyone, Eveline included, believes the killers are after Lilith.</li>
<li><strong>Contio is the noise.</strong> He bribed a guard to snatch the strongbox because he thinks the pearls are in it. He's greedy and loud, but he sends no killers.</li>
<li><strong>Lord Decimus is behind it all.</strong> Octavia's younger brother arranged Cassian's marriage decades ago. He doesn't want to lead the family; he wants it broken apart so his branch can take the biggest share. Nothing in this adventure proves it.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The Valtair family</h3>
<p>The Valtairs descend from <strong>Saraphina</strong>, a founder of Silverwood, and her husband Victor. Saraphina's bargain with a demon for the black pearls had a price: Victor's descendants are all born <strong>tieflings</strong>, and only Victor's blood can wake the pearls.</p>

<ul>
<li>They raise the dead as undead with <strong>their own souls</strong>: not true resurrection.</li>
<li>They answer only to <strong>Victor Valtair's blood</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>They feed on the user's life.</strong> A fresh body costs a little; an old one can kill.</li>
</ul>
<h3>The pearls and attunement</h3>
<p>A pearl starts to <strong>attune</strong> to whoever has it <strong>from the first touch</strong>. No rest is needed, and the bearer may not even notice.</p>
<p><strong>Anyone not of Victor's blood</strong> who touches a pearl knows at once that something is wrong: it is cold, and it seems to be tasting them. They make a <strong>DC 16 Constitution saving throw</strong> there and then. On a failure, their <strong>Strength score drops by 1d4</strong>.</p>
<p>While the pearl stays in their possession, they repeat the save <strong>every hour</strong>. At <strong>0 Strength they die</strong>. Lost Strength returns after a Long Rest once they are rid of the pearl. Nobody outside the bloodline can use a pearl's power.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>Adventure outline</h3>

<h3>The Guild</h3>
<p>The party has been recommended to the Adventurers' Guild in Valenne, the capital, for one reason above all: <strong>none of them has any connection to House Valtair.</strong></p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The Guild clerk doesn't hand you the job. She slides a sealed letter across the counter with two fingers, as if it might stain them.</em></p>
<p><em>"Private commission. House Valtair. Generous terms, paid through us. They asked for people with no ties to the family. None. Not a cousin's maid, not a debt, not a drink once with a footman." She looks at each of you in turn. "I said you'd do. Don't make me a liar."</em></p>
<p><em>"Pearl money, the Valtairs. Half the fine necklaces in this city went through their shops once. Folk say there's a string of black pearls in that house worth more than the street it stands on. Lucky, they say. Or cursed. Depends who's buying the drinks."</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>The letter</strong> (Handout 2) gives an address on the hill and asks the party to present themselves that afternoon.</p>
<h3>The Valtair house</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The Valtair house stands behind a high wall in the oldest quarter of Valenne, all pale stone and tall windows, with gardens kept so neat they look afraid to grow. Guards in grey and silver watch you all the way up the drive. The steward leads you through hallways lined with portraits: horned, handsome, unsmiling Valtairs, every one of them watching you go by.</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>Mr Ashby</strong>, the steward, says only: <em>"Her ladyship is expecting you. She tires easily. She will not admit it. Please don't make her."</em></p></div><div class="flow-col"><div class="gm-note"><h4>Playing Octavia</h4><p>Economical, dry, in complete control of the room. <p><em>Read Octavia's speech as written.</em></p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p>Lady Octavia Valtair sits beside a tall window overlooking the estate gardens. Although visibly frail, her eyes remain sharp and attentive. She studies each of you before speaking.</p>
<p>"Ah. The Adventurers' Guild. Good. Do come in. I trust they've sent me people of competence rather than merely enthusiasm."</p>

<p>"I shall dispense with the pleasantries. My physicians inform me that I have very little time remaining. A few days, perhaps. I find their estimates tiresome, but on this occasion, I suspect they are correct."</p>
<p>A dry cough interrupts her. She carefully folds her handkerchief before continuing.</p>
<p>"Upon my passing, certain matters must be attended to. Matters which require individuals with no connection whatsoever to my family."</p></blockquote></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Valtair Inheritance</span><span>5</span></div>

<h3>Warning signs</h3>
<p>Use two or three of these on the journey.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The same rider twice:</strong> grey cloak, good horse, never close.</li>
<li><strong>Cassian's coachman:</strong> Cassian lends the family his own coachman "for their safety". He asks which road they "prefer" before anyone has said.</li>
<li><strong>The ill ostler:</strong> at the last stop, the usual ostler is suddenly "sick", and the man in his place has a soldier's hands.</li>
<li><strong>Lilith's nightmare:</strong> the night before, she wakes the camp crying. She dreamed her grandmother stood at the foot of her bed, saying nothing.</li>
</ul>
