The players may suspect that within minutes. It is about <strong>proving how he has made innocent people choose evil while believing they chose freely</strong>, learning the price of undoing it, and deciding what kind of justice they will pursue.</p></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Singing Bone</span><span>4</span></div>






\page
<div class="running" style="--accent:#51584e"><span>The Singing Bone</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns " style="--accent:#284f49"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><p data-source="SB-B0020" data-audience="GM">Centuries ago, during the Blood-Song Reign, a gifted bard saved the realm by turning an invading army aside with music. The noble who employed him feared the bard's influence and falsely accused him of treason. The bard was gagged and executed. In his final letter he asked his husband, a master instrument-maker of the Vaelori line, to make one last song from his bones, so that truth might survive him. Grieving and enraged, the husband carved the Singing Bone, a bone flute bound with silver and emotion-laden runes. The bard's final wish was true; the husband's act was not pure. In the making, his love became vengeance. The instrument's gift curdled into a curse: it could place false convictions inside a listener's mind and leave the listener believing those convictions were freely chosen.</p><p data-source="SB-B0021" data-audience="GM">The ancient noble who betrayed the bard founded the line that became House Norell. <strong>Vessa Norell descends from the betrayer, not from Lord Vale.</strong> The instrument-maker's bloodline continued under several names. <strong>Vale and his cousin Lyrra Thorne descend from the Vaelori maker.</strong> The blood of the maker can awaken and direct the flute. The blood of the betrayer alone can discharge the original oath through a genuine, public admission of wrongdoing and renunciation of the benefits inherited from it. This distinction is fundamental: the maker line and the betrayer line are separate, and neither character is secretly the other's parent.</p><p data-source="SB-B0022" data-audience="GM">Lord Vale entered the court of Duskwatch under a poorly documented noble claim. He used the flute to change the judgment of Lord Vikron Norell, Vessa's father, then performed a private bloodline ritual that strengthened his hold on the instrument. The ritual hastened Vikron's death, which was recorded as a death in his sleep. Vale assumed power by controlling witnesses, officials and guards. He has not hypnotised every resident of Veldenhollow. Some are afraid, some profit from his rule, and some genuinely believe his lies. The last category is the most disturbing.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><p data-source="SB-B0023" data-audience="GM"><strong>What ends the evil? Three different victories are possible.</strong></p><ol data-source="SB-B0024" data-audience="GM"><li><strong>Defeat or disarm Vale:</strong> His immediate control over Veldenhollow ends. People regain the capacity to question him, but old false memories and inherited grievances may linger. The flute remains dangerous if it survives.</li><li><strong>Silence or physically shatter the instrument:</strong> An informed musician can suppress its voice; a difficult counter-harmony can shatter its body. Either prevents Vale from playing it, but destroying the <em>object</em> does not automatically settle the ancient vow. Residual false memories require time, witness and care to unwind. A broken relic cannot be played again, though the unresolved curse remains a possible future echo, not an automatic new villain.</li><li><strong>Vessa's true renunciation:</strong> Before people who have suffered from the lie, Vessa freely names her ancestor's betrayal, clears the murdered bard and renounces the title and inherited claim gained through that betrayal. The ancient curse ends, the flute turns to dust wherever it is, and active enchantments founded on its song break. Memories are not instantly rewritten into perfect truth; the survivors must still confront what happened.</li></ol><p data-source="SB-B0025" data-audience="GM">Vessa is innocent of her ancestor's crime. The adventure must <strong>never imply she deserves punishment</strong>. The choice is whether she is willing to give up a benefit created by that crime to release others from its consequence. She may refuse. The characters must be able to achieve meaningful, if imperfect, endings without forcing her.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Singing Bone</span><span>5</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#51584e"><span>The Singing Bone</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns " style="--accent:#284f49"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3 data-source="SB-B0051" data-audience="GM" id="sb-b0051">Rules that settle the curse</h3><p data-source="SB-B0052" data-audience="GM"><strong>The Singing Bone's sustained rewriting of convictions</strong> normally requires repeated exposure, careful words and a receptive victim. Vale's history of coercion therefore cannot be replicated by a one-action combat attack. A person who has been misled does not automatically recover their true memory when the instrument is removed. Who studies the note? Who watches the door? Who protects the witness? Who engineers the exit? Who decides what happens to the flute?</em> These are opportunities, never character-class requirements. In the archive, three research stations support pairs. For five characters use Vale <strong>185 HP</strong>, one guard and one Virtuoso. For four, use Vale <strong>165 HP</strong>, one guard, and the Virtuoso only if the group is well equipped or prepared; reduce Legendary Resistance to <strong>1/day</strong>. If a six-character group has unusually optimised damage output, improve Vale's positioning and use the gallery defensively rather than silently inflating his hit points midway through the fight.</p><h3 data-source="SB-B0343" data-audience="GM" id="sb-b0343">Running the closing return</h3><p data-source="SB-B0344" data-audience="GM">Before the players leave the town, ask them what they do about <strong>Elion</strong>, <strong>Vessa</strong>, the <strong>Virtuoso</strong> if rescued, the <strong>town's leadership</strong> and the <strong>flute</strong>. Their answers supply the five paragraphs of an epilogue. Then return to Vannor's oak desk and ask each character for one sentence of their account. Pay the promised reward if they bring credible corroboration. Close on silence, or on the unsettling three-note echo only if the curse is still unresolved.</p></div></div></div><div data-company-watermark="last-page" style="position:absolute;left:50%;top:50%;transform:translate(-50%,-50%);width:220pt;height:220pt;opacity:0.04;pointer-events:none;z-index:3"><img alt="" style="width:220pt!important;height:220pt!important" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/singing-bone/img/02-img-75c939b3.png"></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Singing Bone</span><span>52</span></div>
