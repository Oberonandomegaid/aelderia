Under the lake lies a drowned monolith, and inside the monolith, something has been waiting a very long time.</p><h3>What is really happening</h3><p>Before THK, an archmage of a vanished civilisation learned to eat magic: spells, the air itself, the gifts of everyone near him. It made him all but deathless. His peers could not beat him with magic, so they tricked him into a stone prison that starves him. Villagers later flooded the valley and drowned the stone without knowing. Water and ice have cracked it. Tonight, Lord Xanthos’s own power gets too close, and the archmage feeds.</p></div><div><h3>Where it leads</h3><ul><li><strong>Act I:</strong> the dragon on the ice, a confused pack of winter wolves, and Lord Xanthos meeting an old acquaintance.</li><li><strong>Act II:</strong> under the ice, into the monolith. <h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>A civilisation of mages.</strong> Long before THK, a people lived here among whom nearly everyone had innate magic. One of their greatest archmages, <strong>Sennoch</strong>, a visionary, slowly abandoned every limit. He experimented on living people until he found how to <strong>absorb magic</strong>: hostile spells, the magic in the air, the gifts of the people around him. He turned it into life. It made him almost impossible to kill, and almost impossible to hurt with magic.</li>
<li><strong>The trick.</strong> His fellow archmages realised their own magical world had no defence against him. So they lied to him, lured him into a monolith built to starve him of magic, and sealed him in, conscious and hungry, forever.</li>
<li><strong>The talking stone.</strong> For thousands of years the monolith stood alone in open fields. Villagers called it the talking stone: it spoke, and anything thrown at it bounced back. Sennoch learned each new language by listening to them.</li>
<li><strong>The reservoir.</strong> A few generations ago, in a terrible drought, the villagers dammed the river and flooded the valley. The stone went under. <strong>Water and ice have cracked it</strong>. Sennoch didn’t do that; time did. Magic has begun to seep in through the cracks, pulling at everything magical for miles like a weight dropped onto silk.</li>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><span>The Monolith Beneath the Ice</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:160px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/monolith-beneath-ice/img/banner-setting.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The setting</h3>
<p><strong>Hobb’s Tarn</strong> is a village of slate roofs and peat smoke in a high valley, a mile below a long frozen reservoir, the <strong>Tarn</strong>, held back by an old stone dam. In winter the ice is a yard thick, and the village fishes through holes and skates to market.</p>
<p>For six days, a <strong>silver dragon</strong> has stood out in the middle of the ice. She doesn’t eat their sheep. She doesn’t burn anything. She just stands there, staring down, and every so often she raises her tail and brings it down on the ice with a crack you can hear in the next valley.</p>
<p>The village is terrified.</p>
<h3>Running it</h3>

<li><strong>Six characters of level 5</strong>, 2024 rules. The level-5 pre-built characters are at the back.</li>
<li><strong>You need:</strong> the map of the frozen reservoir (page 16), Granny Mabb’s sketch (the handout), and something to mark cracks in the ice.</li>
<li><strong>Pacing:</strong> Act I about 60 minutes, Act II about 55, Act III about 75.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>Scaling</h3>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>A <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>, five or six levels above the party, measured and aristocratic, with the dry humour of a man who has been right too often. Here he is an archmage, and looks it. Ask his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> And you are, presumably, the dragon-slayers. How brave.”</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Says he’s doing:</strong> finding out what is draining magic from the valley.</li>
<li><strong>Privately:</strong> fascinated by a prison that has held for thousands of years.</li>
<li><strong>What he doesn’t know:</strong> what is inside. He recognises the monolith’s protective work as similar to things he has built. He has never heard of Sennoch.</li>
<li><strong>In Act III:</strong> he is badly drained and Gelmira carries him away. He gives one warning, then he is gone.</li>
</ul></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Monolith Beneath the Ice</span><span>4</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><span>The Monolith Beneath the Ice</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:130px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/monolith-beneath-ice/img/banner-act1.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h2>Act I — The Dragon on the Ice</h2>
<p><em>About 60 minutes.</em></p>
<h3>The contract</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The reeve of Hobb’s Tarn is a thin man in three coats, all of them too big, and he cannot stop looking up the valley.</em></p>
<p><em>“Six days she’s been out there. Six days. Staring at the ice like she can see through it. And then BANG, with that tail, and the whole Tarn rings like a bell.” He swallows. “We want her gone. We’ll pay. Whatever it takes. Kill her, drive her off, I don’t care how.”</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>Reeve Aldwin Cotter</strong> offers <strong>150 gp each</strong>, which is most of the village’s savings. He believes the dragon wants to break the dam and drown them all.</p>
<h3>Asking around</h3>
<p>The villagers’ stories don’t agree. Let the players find that out:</p>
<ul>
<li>The <strong>fishermen</strong> say she hasn’t touched a single sheep. “She just <em>looks</em>.”</li>
<li>The <strong>blacksmith’s boy</strong> swears she spoke to him. She asked what used to stand in the valley before the water. He ran.</li>
<li><strong>Granny Mabb Thorley</strong>, ninety and sharp as a tack, says nothing at first. If anyone sits with her and listens (no roll needed, just patience), she says: “<em>My gran said there was a stone in that valley. A talking stone. Nobody went near it. Then they made the lake.</em>”</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The road up the valley</h3><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:170px;margin:4px 0 8px"><img alt="Winter wolves drawn to the pull" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/monolith-beneath-ice/img/wolves.jpg" style="object-position:50% 45%"></figure><p><strong>Optional, 15–20 minutes.</strong> Skip it if time is short.</p>
<p>Halfway up the valley, <strong>two winter wolves</strong> come down out of the snow, not hunting: confused. They have felt the pull too, the way a compass feels north, and it has dragged them out of the high passes. They are hungry, frightened and snappish.</p>
<p><strong>About 1,400 XP</strong>: well under Low for this party. They can be fought, or:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Calmed.</strong> Food and a steady voice (DC 13 Wisdom (Animal Handling)): they back off and slink away.</li>
<li><strong>Talked to.</strong> They speak Common, roughly. “<em>Something calls. Under the water. We don’t like it.</em>”</li>
<li><strong>Driven off.</strong> Fire, noise, or one good hit each: they flee.</li>
</ul>
<p>Either way, they foreshadow the truth: <strong>something under the ice is calling to magical creatures</strong>, and the dragon is just another one of them.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Monolith Beneath the Ice</span><span>5</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><span>The Monolith Beneath the Ice</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The dragon</h3><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:260px"><img alt="Gelmira." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/monolith-beneath-ice/img/gelmira.jpg" style="object-position:50% 25%"></div><figcaption>Gelmira.</figcaption></figure><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>She is enormous, and silver, and absolutely still, standing in the middle of the frozen Tarn with her head lowered almost to the ice, as if listening at a door. Frost has gathered on her wings.</em></p>
<p><em>One eye, pale as winter sky, swivels to look at you. She does not move anything else.</em></p>
<p><em>“Dragon-slayers,” she says, in a voice like ice settling. “How very tiresome.”</em></p></blockquote></div><div class="flow-col"><p><strong>Gelmira</strong> is an old silver dragon: curious, patient, dry, and wholly uninterested in the village’s fears. She is here because she felt something pulling at her own magic, and she wants to know what. She is <strong>not hurt, not weakened, and not guarding anything</strong>.</p>
<p><strong>Talking to her</strong> works best. She explains, if asked politely: “<em>Something under this ice is drinking. Not water: magic. It pulls at me like a stone dropped on silk. I have been trying to find the edge of it.</em>” She doesn’t know what it is.</p>
<p><strong>Attacking her</strong> is a terrible idea, but she doesn’t want to kill anybody. She defends herself (Paralyzing Breath, a gust of wind, a snap of her jaws), then says, wearily: “<em>Are you quite finished?</em>”</p><div class="gm-note"><h4>If they fight her anyway</h4><p>She is an <b>Adult Silver Dragon</b> from the free rules, far beyond this party. Don’t run it as a real fight. She paralyses the loudest attacker, knocks another flat with a wing, and talks. Nobody should die here. If it goes on, Lord Xanthos arrives early and ends it with a word.</p></div><h3>Lord Xanthos arrives</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>Footsteps crunch on the ice behind you. A man in an archmage’s robe walks out towards the dragon as if she were a horse he had left tied up outside an inn.</em></p>
<p><em>“Gelmira.”</em></p>
<p><em>“Xanthos.” The dragon’s eye narrows. “You’ve aged.”</em></p>
<p><em>“And you have not improved.” He looks down at the ice. “You feel it too, then.”</em></p></blockquote></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Monolith Beneath the Ice</span><span>6</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><span>The Monolith Beneath the Ice</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#3b5a6e"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>Old acquaintances</h3>
<p>They know each other: they have crossed paths over the years, respect each other and irritate each other in roughly equal measure. Let them bicker a little. <strong>Neither says anything about the past</strong>, and Gelmira knows nothing more about him than anyone else does.</p>
<p>Lord Xanthos came because his instruments detected the same pull. He lays a hand on the ice, frowns, and says: “<em>There is something down there. Something old, and well made.</em>”</p>
<h3>The talking stone</h3>
<p>This is where <strong>Granny Mabb’s sketch</strong> comes in. If the party heard her story, she gives them the drawing her grandmother made: give the players the <strong>handout</strong> (page 25). If they didn’t, a villager remembers it and fetches it.</p>
<p>Lord Xanthos studies it a long time. “<em>That is not a monument. That is a prison. I have seen this kind of warding before.</em>” He taps the reflective spiral drawn on its face. “<em>I have… something like it on my own doors.</em>” He doesn’t know what is in it. <p>Lord Xanthos studies the cracks, chooses one, lays his palms flat on the stone and works. It takes several minutes. The party can help: holding lamps, keeping a line clear, fending off curious fish, or steadying him in the current.</p>

<p>Painted and carved, still bright after thousands of years. Let the players work out what they show. Describe them one at a time; any character can try to interpret one (DC 12 Intelligence (History) or (Investigation), or Wisdom (Insight) for the faces).</p>

<p>The ice fight is easiest with a map, but works without. Keep three things in front of the players: <strong>where the cracks are</strong>, <strong>where Sennoch is</strong>, and <strong>which way the sky is</strong>.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The ice</strong> is a white circle with a black hole in the middle. Describe the cracks spreading out from it like a web. Every Hoarfrost Ruin adds a new strand.</li>
<li><strong>Sennoch</strong> floats a few feet above the ice when he moves. When he is knocked down onto it, say so: that’s when the ice can take him.</li>
<li><strong>The mephits</strong> flit in and out of the snow. Count them down aloud.</li>
