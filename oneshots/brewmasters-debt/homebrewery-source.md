<h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>A sickness.</strong> Last winter, Maudra Sallowbrand fell ill: a slow wasting no healer could name or cure. Her husband, <strong>Brewmaster Orrik</strong>, the finest brewer in Kettlecairn, watched her fade.</li>
<li><strong>A visitor.</strong> One night a charming stranger came to his brewhouse: <strong>the Connoisseur</strong>, a fiend. He offered a cure. The price: a pinch of something dark in Orrik’s festival stout, <strong>Lantern Dark</strong>, so that every drinker would give up “a sip” of their soul. A little from many, to save one. Orrik signed.</li>
<li><strong>The lie.</strong> The Connoisseur <strong>caused Maudra’s illness</strong>, to bring Orrik to him. Orrik doesn’t know.</li>
<li><strong>The loophole.</strong> The contract says each fiend may take only <strong>one</strong> sip from each drinker. It doesn’t say how many fiends may come. On the Night of Lanterns, when the boundary of the dead grows thin, the stout will <strong>tear the breach open</strong>, and a swarm will come through.</li>
<li><strong>Maudra is innocent.</strong> She believes her recovery was a miracle. She knows nothing.</li>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#7a4a14"><span>Brewmaster’s Debt</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#7a4a14"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:160px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/brewmasters-debt/img/banner-setting.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The setting</h3>
<p><strong>Kettlecairn</strong> is a dwarf town cut into a hillside above a cold green lake, all copper brewhouses and steaming vats. At its heart is the <strong>Cairn Square</strong>, where the stone cairns of the town’s ancestors stand in a ring, each one hung with the family’s lanterns.</p>
<p><strong>The Night of Lanterns</strong> is the festival of the dead. Dwarves wear masks carved in their ancestors’ faces, tell stories of the departed, play dice and games, and drink. The tradition: <strong>the first cup is raised to the dead; the second is shared with the living.</strong> On this night the boundary between the living and the dead truly grows thin. Old dwarves swear they see their grandparents in the lantern-light.</p>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>A <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>, five or six levels above the party, but young for it, and still a little impatient. Courteous, sharp, and very sure of himself. Ask his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> You may have heard of my family. Most people have.”</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Says he’s doing:</strong> finding out what has been tearing small holes in the world around Kettlecairn, before it tears a big one.</li>
<li><strong>Privately:</strong> fascinated by souls, and by boundaries between worlds.</li>
<li><strong>What he doesn’t know:</strong> the ale. He hasn’t tasted it. He doesn’t drink at festivals.</li>
<li><strong>In Acts II and III:</strong> he holds the breach with everything he has, and the party must protect him.</li>
<li><strong>And something else.</strong> He is fascinated by the soul fragments, and while the breach is open he studies them before the effect fades, all of it disguised as helping. “<em>Bring the wounded to me. I can steady them.</em>” He watches the lights closely, asks victims what they feel, and remembers every answer.</li>

<h3>Arrival</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The road climbs through pines to a hillside full of light. Lanterns hang from every eave and every cairn, hundreds of them, gold and green and red. Somewhere a band is playing something loud with a great many drums. The air smells of woodsmoke, roast pork and malt.</em></p>
<p><em>Lord Xanthos stops at the town gate and looks at a brass instrument in his palm, its needle trembling.</em></p>
<p><em>“Seven tears in three months,” he says. “Small, brief, and all closed now. Something has been coming and going here.” He snaps the instrument shut. “Enjoy the festival. Keep your eyes open. I would like to know who has been leaving the door ajar.”</em></p></blockquote><p>He goes to pay his respects to <strong>Thane Brunhild Ironwhistle</strong> and examine the cairns. The party have the run of the festival until the <strong>Toast to the Dead at midnight</strong>.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:200px"><img alt="Lantern Dark." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/brewmasters-debt/img/stout.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></div><figcaption>Lantern Dark.</figcaption></figure><h3>The drinkers</h3>
<p><strong>The drinkers</strong> (page 22) are the named townsfolk who drink <strong>Lantern Dark</strong> tonight. They are characters, not a checklist: you only need numbers for them when they are in a fight.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Four drink it early</strong>, in Act I, whatever the party do.</li>
<li><strong>Four more drink it at the midnight toast.</strong> If the party stop the stout being served before then, they don’t.</li>
<li><strong>Any character who drinks Lantern Dark</strong> is marked too. Just remember who. <strong>Don’t tell them.</strong></li>
</ul>
<p>Orrik’s stall is the most popular in the square. A character who just asks for “the festival ale” is offered Lantern Dark. Many other brewers are pouring too; <strong>only Orrik’s stout is poisoned</strong>.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>Brewmaster’s Debt</span><span>5</span></div>

<h3>Stopping the ale</h3>
<p>Once the party know, they can act: <strong>tell the Thane</strong> (with evidence, she orders the stall shut), <strong>sabotage the barrels</strong>, or <strong>confront Orrik</strong>. Each works. If the stout stops before midnight, <strong>cross out the four toast drinkers</strong> on the tracker.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>Brewmaster’s Debt</span><span>6</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#7a4a14"><span>Brewmaster’s Debt</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#7a4a14"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>Confronting Orrik</h3><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:230px"><img alt="Brewmaster Orrik Sallowbrand." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/brewmasters-debt/img/orrik.jpg" style="object-position:50% 20%"></div><figcaption>Brewmaster Orrik Sallowbrand.</figcaption></figure><p>If the party confront Orrik with evidence before midnight, he breaks, but not completely.</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The brewmaster’s great hands are shaking. He looks past you, at his wife, laughing with her friends across the square.</em></p>
<p><em>“A sip,” he says hoarsely. “He said a sip. From each of them. They’d never even feel it. A little from many, to keep her. Wouldn’t you?”</em></p>
<p><em>He wipes his face. “I’ll pour it away. Every drop. Just… don’t tell her. Please.”</em></p></blockquote></div><div class="flow-col"><ul>
<li>He admits the pact, the ingredient and the stout. <strong>He believes only one fiend will come</strong> and take one small sip from each drinker. He doesn’t know about the loophole, and he doesn’t know who made Maudra ill.</li>

<h3>When the harvest ends</h3>
<p>You don’t need to have counted every sip. Decide each named drinker’s fate by <strong>how many imps got through the breach with lights</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>None:</strong> everyone recovers.</li>
<li><strong>1–3:</strong> one drinker (your choice, never a child the party protected) is left dimmed for life.</li>
<li><strong>4 or more:</strong> one drinker dies, and another is left dimmed.</li>
</ul>
<p>Anyone the party <strong>protected in a fight</strong> uses their real tracked Exhaustion instead.</p>
