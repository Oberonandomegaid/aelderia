Its corridors loop, its doors open onto oceans, and its ceilings have become floors. The new Lord Xanthos has felt the ripples from Silverwood. He can hold the library together, or find out why it is falling apart, but not both, so he hires the party through the Adventurers’ Guild and goes in with them.</p><h3>What is really happening</h3><p>A lonely nine-year-old orphan, Elsie Mott, found a blank book in the library. Whatever she writes in it comes true, and only she can make it work. She has written herself friends, a family, and whole worlds to play in. Now she is frightened of losing them, and her fear is tearing the library apart.</p></div><div><h3>Where it leads</h3><ul><li><strong>Act I:</strong> the impossible library, clues to a girl and a book, and a very big, very friendly playmate.</li><li><strong>Act II:</strong> three storybook worlds: the Upside-Down Forest, the Kingdom of Make-Believe and the gates of the castle.</li><li><strong>Act III:</strong> the Castle of Forgotten Things, where her fears are real, and the last page only she can write.</li><li><strong>Ending:</strong> every imaginary friend is gone, and Elsie needs a home.</li></ul><p>Built for six level-5 characters in one session of about 3–3½ hours. <h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Elsie Mott</strong> is nine. Her parents died of a fever two winters ago, and she ran away from the town’s foundling house, a cold, grey place. She has been living in the <strong>Free Library of Penwithy</strong>, hiding in the reading rooms after closing. The old librarian knows, and pretends not to.</li>
<li><strong>The Blank Book.</strong> On a high shelf Elsie found a book with empty pages and a brass clasp shaped like a keyhole. She wrote in it: <em>“Once upon a time there was a girl called Elsie and she had a friend.”</em> And she did.</li>
<li><strong>The magic is in the book.</strong> Elsie isn’t a sorceress. But <strong>only she can make it work</strong>. For anyone else, the pages stay blank, and ink slides off them like rain off glass.</li>
<li><strong>Her worlds.</strong> She has written a forest, a kingdom, a castle, a giant knitted bear and a family of storybook people who love her. Each one follows a child’s logic.</li>
<li><strong>Her fear.</strong> She knows someone will take it all away. As grown-ups come closer, her fear shapes the library: corridors twist, nightmares wake. <strong>She isn’t trying to hurt anyone.</strong> But people are getting hurt.</li>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5b4a7a"><span>The Library of Childhood Dreams</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5b4a7a"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:160px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/library-childhood-dreams/img/banner-setting.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The setting</h3>
<p><strong>Penwithy</strong> is a busy market town of cobbled streets and slate roofs. Its pride is the <strong>Free Library</strong>: three floors of oak shelves, a glass dome, reading rooms, and a children’s corner with a painted ceiling of clouds and kites.</p>
<p>For a week, strange things have spilled out of it. Books fluttering down the high street like starlings. A shopkeeper’s cat that started talking, rudely. A tide of warm sea water through the market one morning, with a small, embarrassed whale. At first it was funny. Then the baker’s boy fell <strong>up</strong> into the sky outside the library, and only a chimney stopped him.</p>
<p>The library’s doors are shut now, and inside, it is folding.</p>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>A <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>, five or six levels above the party. He has only lately come into his title, and he is still finding his feet: courteous, watchful, a little stiff, and spending more effort on his spells than he would like. Ask his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> The new one. My predecessor’s shoes are large, and I am still walking about in them.”</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Says he’s doing:</strong> keeping Penwithy from being folded into a book.</li>
<li><strong>Privately:</strong> deeply curious about a book that rewrites the world.</li>
<li><strong>What he can do:</strong> hold the library steady around the party (see “Xanthos’s anchors”, page 5).</li>
<li><strong>What he won’t do:</strong> solve their puzzles, talk to Elsie for them, or use the book. Ever.</li>

<p><em>About 55 minutes.</em></p>
<h3>The Guild</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The Guildmaster’s office smells of pipe smoke and old maps. The man waiting there in a Silverwood cloak turns as you enter, and inclines his head exactly the right amount.</em></p>
<p><em>“Thank you for coming. I am Lord Xanthos. I have been inside the library.” He pauses. “It is changing faster than I can follow. I can hold it together, or I can find out why it is coming apart. I cannot do both. I need people who can do the second.”</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>Guildmaster Harrow Quaile</strong> offers <strong>100 gp each</strong> from the town, and Xanthos adds the same. Xanthos tells them what he knows: the magic is centred deep in the library; it feels <strong>old, and strangely young at the same time</strong>; and something in there is <strong>afraid</strong>.</p>
<h3>The doors</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The library’s great doors swing open on their own. Inside, the entrance hall goes up much further than the building does outside. Staircases climb into each other. A shelf of atlases floats past at head height, turning its pages. From somewhere far above comes a child’s laugh, and then, quickly, silence.</em></p></blockquote></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>Xanthos's anchors</h3>
<p>Xanthos walks with the party and keeps reality steady. <strong>In each act he can do three of these</strong>, one at a time. The players can ask; you decide what he can manage.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hold the floor.</strong> A room stops shifting for a minute.</li>
<li><strong>Close a door.</strong> A doorway into somewhere deadly (an ocean, a drop, a fire) slams shut.</li>
<li><strong>Shield.</strong> One creature takes no damage from one hazard.</li>
<li><strong>Open a way.</strong> A short passage that wasn’t there, for a few breaths.</li>
</ul><div class="gm-note"><h4>What he never does</h4><p>Solve a puzzle. Win a fight. Find Elsie for them. Speak to her for them. Touch the book’s pages. If players lean on him, he says: “<em>I am holding up the ceiling. Do try to do the rest.</em>”</p></div><h3>Signs of a child</h3>
<p>Let the party find these as they go. Each points deeper in:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Biscuit crumbs</strong> and a tiny shoe, trailing up a staircase that goes sideways.</li>
<li><strong>Drawings</strong> on the backs of library cards: a girl, a bear, a house with smoke. <em>“ME. MISTER B. HOME.”</em></li>
<li><strong>A nest</strong> in the children’s corner: blankets, a candle stub, a doll.</li>
<li><strong>Mistress Odile Brask</strong>, the old librarian, stuck on a staircase that loops (see next page).</li>
</ul></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Library of Childhood Dreams</span><span>5</span></div>

<p>Run the library as a handful of <strong>rooms that won’t stay put</strong>. Use any or all:</p>

<li><strong>The Ceiling Room.</strong> Gravity flips every round. DC 12 Acrobatics to land well; fail and take 1d6. Books fall up.</li>
<li><strong>The Sea Door.</strong> A reading-room door opens onto open ocean, which begins pouring in (DC 13 Athletics to shut it, or Xanthos <em>closes a door</em>).</li>
<li><strong>The Hall of Fluttering.</strong> A <strong>flock of brass letter-openers</strong> and <strong>two hungry rugs</strong>, written in a frightened moment. <strong>They are not playing.</strong> About 1,200 XP: a Low fight, but a real one.</li>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5b4a7a"><span>The Library of Childhood Dreams</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5b4a7a"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:120px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/library-childhood-dreams/img/banner-end.jpg" style="object-position:50% 55%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h2>Ending — Once Upon a Time</h2><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>Elsie dips the pen, and writes, slowly, in big careful letters. When she reaches the end, she looks up at her friends.</em></p>
<p><em>Mister Bramblecoat waves a woolly paw. The Jam Queen blows her a kiss. And then, gently, like breath on a cold window, they fade.</em></p>
<p><em>The walls fold away. The castle is gone. You are standing in the children’s corner of the Free Library of Penwithy, under a painted ceiling of clouds and kites, and it is morning, and a small girl is crying into the cardigan of the person nearest to her.</em></p></blockquote><h3>What returns, and what doesn't</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Everything she wrote is gone</strong>: the worlds, the creatures, the friends. <strong>None stays.</strong></li>
<li><strong>The library and town</strong> are exactly as they were.</li>
<li><strong>Real injuries remain.</strong> Anyone hurt is still hurt; <strong>anyone who died is still dead.</strong> Ordinary healing can help; the book cannot.</li>
<li><strong>Xanthos takes the book.</strong> He closes its brass clasp and wraps it in silk without reading a word. “<em>Somewhere safe,</em>” he says. He does not say where.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>If she was forced</h3>
<p>If the party used magic, threats or force, Elsie wrote it, but she won’t look at them. She clings to Odile, or to Xanthos’s cloak, never to them. <strong>She survives, and she will be all right in time.</strong> The party will not be part of her life.</p>
<h3>Where Elsie goes</h3>
<p><strong>The party help decide.</strong> Xanthos will see it done, whatever they choose. Options:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Mistress Odile Brask</strong>, the old librarian, who let her stay. She would like nothing better.</li>
<li><strong>Guildmaster Quaile’s sister</strong>, a baker with four noisy children and room for one more.</li>
<li><strong>One of the party</strong>, if a player wants that.</li>
<li><strong>The foundling house.</strong> Safe, fed and grey. Elsie goes white at the thought.</li>
</ul>
<h3>Rewards</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>200 gp each</strong>: 100 from the town, 100 from Lord Xanthos.</li>
<li><strong>A library card</strong>, for life, signed by Mistress Odile.</li>
<li><strong>A button</strong>, if they played with Mister Bramblecoat. It stays. It is only a button now, but Elsie will always know it.</li>
</ul></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Library of Childhood Dreams</span><span>19</span></div>

<h3>The fixed rules</h3>
<ul>
<li>Elsie isn’t evil. No boss fight with her.</li>
<li>Only Elsie can write the ending. Taking the book does nothing.</li>
<li>Mister Bramblecoat never takes anyone below 1 HP. At 0 he is unconscious, not dead.</li>
<li>Everything else in the library can really hurt and kill.</li>
<li>Every creation vanishes at the end. Real harm stays.</li>
<li>Xanthos protects (three anchors per act, one in Act III) and never uses the book.</li>
</ul>
