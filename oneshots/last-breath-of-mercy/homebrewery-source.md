Yet the town of Hollowmere, close to where it began, has not lost a single person to it. Lord Xanthos brings the party through the barrier around the town to find out why, and whether its protection could save anyone else.</p><h3>What is really happening</h3><p>Hollowmere’s lords made a real pact with Morveth, goddess of pestilence: one townsperson every three days, and the plague cannot touch the town. Morveth keeps her word. But the lords lie about the rest. They tell the town that Morveth herself chooses the victims in a holy order. She doesn’t. The lords choose, in secret, and they choose the people who are inconvenient. When a warm, welcoming baker is chosen for talking to the party, her little girl comes to them for help.</p></div><div><h3>Where it leads</h3><ul><li><strong>Act I:</strong> the briefing, the barrier, a town that should be dead, and a baker and her daughter.</li><li><strong>Act II:</strong> “They took Mummy.” The guards, the rescue, and the proof that the list is a lie.</li><li><strong>Act III:</strong> sunset in the square: the lords, the crowd, the elite guards, and a choice nobody can make for the players.</li><li><strong>Ending:</strong> whatever the party choose, and what it costs.</li></ul><p>Built for six level-5 characters in one session of about 3–3½ hours. <h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>The plague is Morveth’s answer to an insult.</strong> A scholar decided the gods exist only because people believe in them, and tried to erase Morveth from memory to prove it. She struck him with a magical plague. Those around him caught it, and it spread on its own. Fear has made her stronger. She is content to let it run, and she will not push it further.</li>
<li><strong>It will end within a year.</strong> Morveth said so, and it is true. Nine months have passed.</li>
<li><strong>Hollowmere’s pact is real.</strong> The ruling lords bargained with her: <strong>one townsperson every three days</strong>, and a barrier keeps the plague out. Outsiders don’t count. Miss a sacrifice and the protection simply stops.</li>
<li><strong>The holy order is a lie.</strong> Morveth never said who should die. The lords pick the victims, in secret, and tell the town that the goddess chose. They pick people who argue, complain or ask questions. They also tell the town that over nine in ten die outside the barrier. The truth is nearer half or two thirds.</li>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:170px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/last-breath-of-mercy/img/banner-setting.jpg" style="object-position:50% 50%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The setting</h3>
<p><strong>Hollowmere</strong> is a market town of two thousand souls in a green valley, two days’ ride from where the plague began. It has a river, a mill, a chapel to half a dozen gods, a town square with a well, and the Merrows’ hall on the hill above.</p>
<p>For nine months it has been wrapped in a faint, shimmering dome, like heat over a road. Outside, the villages are emptying. Inside, children play in the square, the market opens every morning, and the bells ring every third evening at sunset.</p>
<p>Nobody talks about why the bells ring.</p>
<p>In the middle of the square stands a new shrine of grey stone with Morveth’s emblem carved over the door: <strong>a decaying rat with a dying plant growing from its body</strong>. On its wall is a stone tablet: <strong>the Order of Morveth</strong>, a list of names. Every third evening, someone on that list walks into the shrine at sunset, and does not walk out.</p>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>A <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>, five or six levels above the party, measured, authoritative and an aristocrat to the bone. Ask his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> I should have thought that obvious.”</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Says he’s doing:</strong> finding out whether Hollowmere’s protection could stop the plague elsewhere. He means it.</li>
<li><strong>Privately:</strong> the plague is killing whole bloodlines, and that matters to him more than he will say.</li>
<li><strong>In Hollowmere:</strong> the barrier caps his magic, and he spends most of the adventure hunting what causes it. He is never far, and never in charge of the party’s choices.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>He will not make the decision for them.</strong> If asked what they should do, he says: “<em>That is for you, and for them. I am not their lord.</em>”</p>
<p>Voice: a physician who has seen a great deal of dying and has stopped pretending to be gentle about it.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>4</span></div>

<p><em>About 50 minutes.</em></p>
<h3>The briefing</h3>
<p>The party travel with Lord Xanthos through empty villages to a hilltop overlooking Hollowmere. Below, the town glitters inside its dome. Read the briefing as written: it is his.</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>“As you are no doubt aware, a great pestilence has ravaged our lands for the past nine months.</em></p>
<p><em>“I have established that its origins lie with a particularly foolish scholar who, in his arrogance, attempted to challenge Morveth herself. His efforts succeeded only in provoking the goddess’s wrath, and countless lives have been lost as a consequence.</em></p>
<p><em>“Yet there is something rather curious.</em></p>
<p><em>“A town situated remarkably close to the source of the outbreak has, according to every account I have obtained, suffered not a single death from the pestilence.</em></p>
<p><em>“Nine months. Not one recorded death.</em></p>
<p><em>“I intend to discover why.</em></p>
<p><em>“If whatever protects these people can be understood, perhaps it can be used to halt the spread of this disease elsewhere.</em></p>
<p><em>“That, my friends, is why I have brought you here.”</em></p></blockquote></div><div class="flow-col"><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:360px"><img alt="Lord Xanthos, 975 THK." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/last-breath-of-mercy/img/xanthos-portrait.jpg" style="object-position:50% 0%"></div><figcaption>Lord Xanthos, 975 THK.</figcaption></figure><h3>Questions they'll ask</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>“Who are you?”</strong> “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> I should have thought that obvious.”</li>
<li><strong>“What protects them?”</strong> “If I knew, I would not need you.”</li>
<li><strong>“Is it safe to go in?”</strong> “Safer than staying out here.”</li>
<li><strong>Pay:</strong> 200 gp each, on his word. He finds the question faintly vulgar.</li>
</ul>
<p>Note what he says: <strong>no recorded deaths from the plague.</strong> He chose those words with care. Let the players notice, or not.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>5</span></div>

Lord Xanthos lays his palm against it. Light runs out from his hand in thin cracks, and a doorway opens, just wide enough for one.</em></p>
<p><em>You step through. Behind you, the door closes as if it had never been.</em></p>
<p><em>Lord Xanthos stands very still. His head turns slowly, as though listening to something only he can hear.</em></p>
<p><em>“She is watching,” he says quietly. Then, more sharply: “And something here has clipped my wings.”</em></p></blockquote><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:180px;margin:6px 0 10px"><img alt="Lord Xanthos opens a passage through the barrier" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/last-breath-of-mercy/img/barrier.jpg" style="object-position:50% 45%"></figure><div class="gm-note"><h4>The magic cap</h4><p>Inside the barrier, nobody can cast a spell above the <b>highest spell level any party member can cast</b> (3rd level for this party). The party notice nothing different. Lord Xanthos notices a great deal. He goes off to find the cause, and is gone for most of the next two days.</p></div></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>A town that should be dead</h3>
<p>Give the party the run of Hollowmere for a day and a half. Let them wander, ask questions and eat. It should feel almost normal, which is what makes it wrong.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The market</strong> is open. Prices are high and everyone is polite. Nobody makes eye contact for long.</li>
<li><strong>The chapel</strong> has fresh flowers on every altar except one: a bare shelf where something was taken down.</li>
<li><strong>The shrine</strong> in the square is new, grey and silent. The tablet on its wall lists ninety-two names; ninety have been struck through.</li>
<li><strong>The bells</strong> ring every third evening at sunset. The party arrive on the morning after the last ringing. The next is in <strong>three days</strong>.</li>
</ul>
<p>If anyone asks about the shrine, the tablet or the bells, townsfolk go quiet, then say the same words, as if taught: “<em>It’s the Order of Morveth. She chose. We’re grateful.</em>”</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>6</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>The Tallows</h3><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:270px"><img alt="Bryony and Pip Tallow." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/last-breath-of-mercy/img/bryony.jpg" style="object-position:50% 22%"></div><figcaption>Bryony and Pip Tallow.</figcaption></figure><p>On the first morning, the party are nearly knocked over by the smell of hot bread, and then by <strong>Bryony Tallow</strong>.</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>A big woman with flour to her elbows and a laugh like a dropped tray barrels out of the bakery. “Strangers! Real ones! Oh, look at you, you’re thin as rakes, the lot of you.” She presses a warm loaf into the nearest pair of hands and looks the tallest of you up and down, appreciatively. “Well. Hollowmere’s looking up.”</em></p>
<p><em>Behind her skirts, a small girl with a scab on her chin and a wooden sword glares at you suspiciously.</em></p>
<p><em>“That’s Pip,” says Bryony. “She’s going to be a knight. Pip, say hello.”</em></p>
<p><em>“No,” says Pip.</em></p></blockquote></div><div class="flow-col"><p><strong>Bryony Tallow</strong> is warm, loud, generous and a terrible flirt, and none of it is an act. She lost her husband two winters ago and decided that grief was not going to get the bread baked. She feeds half the street. She believes in the pact the way she believes in rain: it is simply how things are. She is grateful the goddess spared her, and she sends a loaf to every family on the list.</p>
<p><strong>Pip Tallow</strong> is seven, fierce, and suspicious of everyone except her mother. She wants to be a knight. She will warm up to anyone who takes her sword seriously.</p>
<h3>Make them liked</h3>
<p>This is the most important scene in the adventure. Give the Tallows time. Let Bryony feed the party, tease them, ask about the outside world (and be horrified by the answers). Let Pip challenge someone to a duel. Let a player promise Pip something.</p>
<p>They must matter to the players before day three. Everything in Acts II and III depends on it.</p><div class="gm-note"><h4>Talking to outsiders</h4><p>Bryony tells the party, cheerfully, things nobody else will: that the list always seems to pick people who annoyed the lords, that old Tam the tanner was on it a week after he refused the new tithe, and that she “wouldn’t want to be on Lady Merrow’s bad side.” She laughs it off. <div class="running" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>Sunset: what happens</h3>
<p>When the tracker runs out, the sun is down. <strong>If one genuine townsperson has gone into the shrine and died, the pact holds.</strong> If not, it ends. Whatever the party did, decide which of these happened. <strong>The players' choices decide it, not you.</strong></p><p><strong>Who might go?</strong> Nobody is required. Some who might, if the story goes their way:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bryony</strong>, who may still choose it, truth or not.</li>
<li><strong>Old Wat Gessling</strong>, a shepherd near ninety, who has stood at every sunset and is tired. He may step up if the crowd turns: “<em>Let it be someone who’s had his life.</em>”</li>
<li><strong>Jory Pellet</strong>, in shame.</li>
<li><strong>Lord Aldous Merrow</strong> himself. If the crowd has turned and someone puts it to him, he may go, broken, almost relieved. He is a townsperson; it counts.</li>
<li><strong>Lady Merrow</strong> never volunteers.</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The party can’t.</strong> If one tries, the shrine simply does not take them. Nothing happens. The sun keeps setting.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>If nobody goes</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The sun slips below the hills. The bells stop.</em></p>
<p><em>For a long moment, nothing happens.</em></p>
<p><em>Then the shimmer overhead thins, and fades, like breath on cold glass. For the first time in nine months, an ordinary wind blows through Hollowmere, and somewhere, out in the dark, a dog begins to bark.</em></p></blockquote><p>No curse comes. No storm. <strong>The plague arrives slowly</strong>, over days and weeks, the way it arrived everywhere else. The town has perhaps three months until the year Morveth promised is up.</p>
<h3>If someone goes</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>They walk up the shrine steps and through the dark door. Nobody follows. The square holds its breath.</em></p>
<p><em>After a while, the barrier overhead brightens, very slightly, and settles. The bells ring once more, softly, and then stop.</em></p></blockquote><p>The pact holds. Hollowmere is safe for three more days, and then it all begins again, until the year is out.</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>20</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#5b4a3a"><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:150px"><img alt="" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/last-breath-of-mercy/img/banner-end.jpg" style="object-position:50% 55%"></figure><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h2>Ending — After Sunset</h2><p>There is no right ending. Here are three that might happen. Yours may be none of them.</p>
<p><strong>The Price Is Paid.</strong> Someone goes. Hollowmere stays protected. The square empties in silence. Whether the Merrows still rule depends on what the crowd saw.</p>
<p><strong>The Truth Prevails.</strong> The lie is exposed, the Merrows fall, and a genuine townsperson walks into the shrine of their own free will. The protection holds. The town will choose the rest itself, by lot or by volunteers, in the open. It is still terrible. It is no longer a lie.</p>
<p><strong>Mercy Has a Cost.</strong> Nobody goes. The barrier fades. Nobody died tonight, and some will die because of it, over the coming weeks. The players may stay and help. Lord Xanthos may.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The Merrows</h3>
<p>What happens to them is up to the players and the crowd. Exposed, they might be arrested, exiled, killed, or simply left to live in a town that knows. Lord Merrow may go into the shrine. Lady Merrow will defend herself to the end: “<em>I kept two thousand people alive. Who here can say the same?</em>”</p>
<h3>The Tallows</h3>
<p>If Bryony lives, she bakes. If she doesn’t, Pip keeps the wooden sword. Either way, someone has to look after Pip. Players who made her a promise in Act I should remember it now.</p>
<h3>Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>He doesn’t judge the party’s choice aloud. Before he leaves, he stands at the edge of the barrier, or the place where it was, and looks back at the town.</p>
<p>“<em>Two thousand,</em>” he says, to nobody. “<em>For a handful.</em>” A pause. “<em>I have heard worse bargains. I have made some.</em>” Then he walks out into the plague as if it were weather.</p>
<h3>Rewards</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>200 gp each</strong> from Lord Xanthos, as promised.</li>
<li><strong>The Merrows’ strongbox.</strong> If the Merrows were exposed, the town opens it and pays the strangers who told the truth: <strong>300 gp</strong>. If the night ended badly, it stays shut.</li>
<li><strong>Whatever the town gives</strong>, depending on how it ended: thanks, bread for life, or a long, cold silence.</li>
</ul></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Last Breath of Mercy</span><span>22</span></div>
