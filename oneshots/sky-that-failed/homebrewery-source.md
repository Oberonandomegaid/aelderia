<div class="running" style="--accent:#3d4f6b"><span>The Sky That Failed</span><span>GM ONLY</span></div><div class="pf-body gm-synopsis" style="--accent:#3d4f6b"><h1>GM synopsis</h1><p class="synopsis-deck">The Sky That Failed</p><div class="synopsis-columns"><div><h3>The adventure</h3><p>The party are hired as bodyguards aboard the <strong>Aurelian Splendour</strong>, the most luxurious airship ever built, on its maiden voyage. The owners insist on a scenic detour over the Rolling Hills. The hills move, and so do the ley lines that power the ship. One by one, its systems fail. The airship will crash. Nothing can stop that.</p><h3>What is really happening</h3><p>The ship runs on an ancient ley-line conductor, recovered and bolted onto modern machinery that doesn’t quite fit it. Over the Rolling Hills, the ley lines shift beneath it, cutting the power in and out until the conductor burns out. <strong>The party can’t save the airship. They can save the people aboard it.</strong> How many, who, and at what cost is entirely up to them.</p></div><div><h3>Where it leads</h3><ul><li><strong>Act I:</strong> the guests, the crew, a very silly safety demonstration, and the detour.</li><li><strong>Act II:</strong> the power fails, the captive air elemental breaks loose, and Lord Xanthos becomes a living conductor.</li><li><strong>Act III:</strong> evacuation by bubble, a strongbox or the servants, the valley that turns into a cliff.</li><li><strong>Ending:</strong> survivors land on popping bubbles. Lord Xanthos climbs out of the wreck.</li></ul><p>Built for six level-5 characters in one session of about 3–3½ hours. <h3>What's really going on</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>The Aurelian Splendour is brand new.</strong> A luxury showpiece: velvet, crystal, a ballroom, a string quartet. Its heart is old: an <strong>ancient ley-line conductor</strong>, recovered from a ruin and fitted into new machinery that was never quite made for it.</li>
<li><strong>The Rolling Hills move.</strong> Some drift over seasons, some in minutes, and the ley lines underneath move with them. Nobody knows why. Lord Xanthos knows they are unstable, and has warned against flying over them.</li>
<li><strong>The owners want the view.</strong> The planned route avoids the Hills. The owners demand a scenic detour. The captain gives in.</li>
<li><strong>The power cuts in and out.</strong> Over the Hills, the ley lines keep breaking and reconnecting under the ship. Each break is a jolt; each reconnection is a surge. The old conductor and the new machinery fight each other, and the conductor burns out.</li>
<li><strong>The ship will crash.</strong> It hits a hillside and is destroyed. <strong>No one on the ground is hurt.</strong> Everything else depends on the party.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The fixed rules</h3>

<p>Use the <strong>disaster clock</strong> (page 19) from the end of Act I. It has <strong>five stages</strong>, and it always moves forward:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>The first break:</strong> jolts, spilled wine, “perfectly normal”.</li>
<li><strong>Surges and the elemental:</strong> the lights flare, the elemental breaks loose.</li>
<li><strong>The conductor fails:</strong> the ship drops; Lord Xanthos takes its place.</li>
<li><strong>The descent:</strong> evacuation; the ship is going down.</li>
<li><strong>The valley:</strong> the hills move; impact.</li>
</ol>
<p>Inside stages 3 and 4, track <strong>minutes</strong> on the clock (ten boxes each). Every scene costs boxes. Good ideas, good rolls and good preparation <strong>save</strong> boxes. When the boxes run out, the stage ends, ready or not.</p>
<h3>Rescue tally</h3>
<p>Keep a simple list of <strong>who is saved</strong>. Every named passenger and every group (the servants, the band, the kitchen staff) is either <strong>on a bubble</strong> or <strong>still aboard</strong> when the clock hits stage 5. At the end, anyone still aboard is lost. Anyone on a bubble lands, more or less safely.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>Running it</h3>

<li><strong>Six characters of level 5</strong>, 2024 rules. The level-5 pre-built characters are at the back.</li>
<li><strong>You need:</strong> the cut-away map (page 13), the safety card handout, and the disaster clock.</li>
<li><strong>Pacing:</strong> Act I about 45 minutes, Act II about 70, Act III about 85.</li>

<h3>Playing Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>A <strong>leader first and a wizard second</strong>, five or six levels above the party: young in this body, aristocratic, measured, and dryly witty. He is aboard purely for the ancient conductor. He didn’t choose the party; he is mildly surprised to see them, and doesn’t hide it.</p>
<p>Ask his name: “<strong>Lord Xanthos.</strong> Do try to keep up.”</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Says he’s doing:</strong> observing ancient technology in use.</li>
<li><strong>In the disaster:</strong> he holds the ship up with his own body, and can do nothing else. He never solves the party’s problems for them.</li>
<li><strong>He never explains</strong> why the Hills move. He doesn’t know.</li>
</ul></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Sky That Failed</span><span>4</span></div>

<p>Give the party time to mingle in the grand saloon. Introduce these people. Each will matter when the ship comes down.</p>

<li><strong>Silas Crane</strong>, a jumpy violinist in the quartet, who hates heights.</li>
<li><strong>Ezra Doyle</strong>, a young junior engineer, very proud of the ship.</li>
<li><strong>The servants</strong>: a dozen maids, footmen and cooks, mostly below decks.</li>

<li><strong>The red wheel.</strong> Beside the engine room door: an emergency release that opens the conductor housing. “<em>Only in the most unlikely event.</em>”</li>
<li><strong>The speaking tubes.</strong> Brass tubes in every room connect to the bridge. Blow, then speak.</li>
</ol>

<p>Over dinner, Lady Vantrey announces, with a little clink of her glass, that the ship will be turning south to pass over the <strong>Rolling Hills</strong> at sunset. “<em>The finest view in the kingdom. Our guests deserve nothing less.</em>”</p><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>Lord Xanthos sets down his fork.</em></p>
<p><em>“The ley lines over the Rolling Hills move,” he says, quite pleasantly. “Your ship runs on ley lines. I would not, if I were you.”</em></p>
<p><em>“Nonsense,” says Lord Vantrey. “Captain?”</em></p>
<p><em>Captain Holt looks from one to the other. Then he smiles, a little too widely. “Of course, my lord. A small detour.”</em></p></blockquote><p><strong>The players can push back.</strong> They can argue, find Wenna Pike (who agrees with Xanthos), or even go to the bridge. They can’t stop it: the captain has decided, and the owners own the ship. <strong>But preparing pays off.</strong> Each of these earns <strong>one box</strong> on the stage-3 or stage-4 clock later:</p>
<ul>
<li>Getting Wenna Pike to put her crew on standby.</li>
<li>Having Ezra show them the engine room and the red wheel now.</li>
<li>Telling Miss Pettigrew to keep the children close.</li>
<li>Anything else sensible.</li>
</ul></div><div class="flow-col"><h3>The first jolt</h3><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The sun is going down over a sea of soft green hills. Everyone is at the windows. The quartet is playing something gentle.</em></p>

A spot of turbulence.”</em></p></blockquote><p>A DC 13 Intelligence (Arcana) check, or watching Lord Xanthos’s face, tells a character it isn’t turbulence: the ship’s power cut out entirely, and came back too hard.</p>

<h3>Stage 2: surges</h3>
<p>The jolts come faster. Every few minutes the hum dies, everything floats, and the power slams back in. Chandeliers swing. Lights flare too bright and burst. In the saloon, a table slides into the quartet. Captain Holt stops saying “perfectly normal”.</p>
<h3>The elemental</h3>
<p>Deep in the hull, inside a cage of brass and runes, an <strong>air elemental</strong> has been helping to keep the ship aloft since it was launched. Nobody asked it. The surges crack its cage, and it bursts out into the cargo hold, roaring.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><figure class="tb-pic"><div class="tb-frame" style="height:250px"><img alt="The captive breaks free." src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/sky-that-failed/img/elemental.jpg" style="object-position:50% 30%"></div><figcaption>The captive breaks free.</figcaption></figure><p>It is not evil. <strong>It is a prisoner</strong>, terrified and furious, smashing at the walls to get out. Every time it hits the hull, the ship lurches. The players can handle it in many ways:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Talk to it.</strong> Anyone who speaks Primordial (or uses magic to be understood) can try to calm it: DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion). It wants to be free.</li>
<li><strong>Free it.</strong> Open the cargo doors (DC 12 Strength, or the lever by the hatch). It roars out into the sky, and on its way past, <strong>it lifts the ship for a moment</strong>, out of gratitude or simple wind: the party gain <strong>two boxes</strong> on the stage-3 clock.</li>
<li><strong>Put it back.</strong> Repair the cage (DC 15 Intelligence with tools or Arcana) while others hold it off. Its strength keeps working for the ship: <strong>one box</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Fight it.</strong> It fights back. If it is destroyed, the ship loses its help: <strong>one box fewer</strong>.</li>
</ul></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Sky That Failed</span><span>9</span></div>

Every light on the ship goes out at once.</em></p>
<p><em>And the Aurelian Splendour drops.</em></p>

<p>Move the clock to <strong>stage 3</strong>. Ten boxes. Every scene from here costs boxes.</p>
<p>The way to the engine room runs through the tilting ship (see the map). On the way, at least one of these happens:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The grand staircase</strong> (overleaf).</li>
<li><strong>A jammed bulkhead door</strong>, with a crewman trapped behind it (DC 14 Strength to force it; 1 box).</li>
<li><strong>A burst steam pipe</strong> across the corridor: 3d6 Fire damage to anyone who runs through, or a clever way round.</li>
</ul>
<h3>The living conductor</h3><figure class="tb-banner" style="height:170px;margin:4px 0 8px"><img alt="Lord Xanthos as the living conductor" src="https://oberonandomegaid.github.io/aelderia/oneshots/sky-that-failed/img/conductor.jpg" style="object-position:50% 45%"></figure><p>In the engine room, the ancient conductor is a cracked, blackened crystal in a brass housing, spitting sparks. Wenna Pike is beating at it with a wrench. Lord Xanthos is already rolling up his sleeves.</p>
<p>“<em>It is finished,</em>” he says. “<em>But I am not. Open it, and connect me.</em>”</p></div></div></div><div class="folio-footer"><span>The Sky That Failed</span><span>10</span></div>

<div class="running" style="--accent:#3d4f6b"><span>The Sky That Failed</span><span>GM</span></div><div class="pf-body columns" style="--accent:#3d4f6b"><div class="layout-cols"><div class="flow-col"><h3>Connecting Lord Xanthos</h3>
<p>This is a team task, not one roll. There are <strong>four jobs</strong>, and they can happen at once. Each costs <strong>1 box</strong>, or <strong>2</strong> if it goes badly. Let the players choose who does what:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Open the housing.</strong> The <strong>red wheel</strong> from the safety tour. A player who remembers it just turns it. Otherwise DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation) to find it, or force the housing (DC 15 Strength).</li>

<li><strong>Bind him in.</strong> Copper cables round his wrists and chest, wired to the housing. DC 12 Intelligence (Arcana), or follow Lord Xanthos’s curt instructions.</li>
</ol>
<p>When all four are done, he closes his eyes.</p></div><div class="flow-col"><blockquote data-label="READ ALOUD"><p><em>The cables snap taut. <p><em>The ship stops falling. It does not rise.</em></p>
<p><em>“I can hold her,” he says, very quietly, without opening his eyes. “For a while. He can talk, a little, and that is all. He keeps the ship in the air; the rest is the party’s.</p>
